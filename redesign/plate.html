Exploration that is actually finished.

Load a CSV, an Excel workbook, JSON, Parquet, or read directly from S3-compatible storage. Skyulf profiles the dataset and produces the statistical survey most projects intend to do and abandon after three charts.

A pipeline whose order you can see.

Preprocessing, feature engineering, splitting, and modeling are nodes on a canvas. Sequence is the whole point: leakage is an ordering mistake, and ordering mistakes disappear inside a long notebook.

Training runs in the background through Celery and Redis, or on background threads. Every canvas exports to a Jupyter notebook, full or compact, so the work is never held hostage by the interface that made it.

The long part comes after the score.

A registry with version history, deployment, inference, drift monitoring, audit history, and operational diagnostics live in the same project as the pipeline that produced the model. When production moves, the lineage is already in front of you.

  • IngestionCSV, Excel, JSON, Parquet, and S3-compatible object storage
  • ArtifactsLocal disk or your own S3-compatible storage, with your credentials
  • MetadataSQLite for small deployments, PostgreSQL for larger ones
  • ExecutionCelery with Redis, or background threads
  • DiagnosticsNode-level timings showing which pipeline step is slow

skyulf-core

The engine alone, in scripts and notebooks. Polars-friendly, built on scikit-learn rather than replacing it. Python 3.12+.

# pip install skyulf-core
from skyulf import SkyulfPipeline

pipeline = SkyulfPipeline({
    "preprocessing": [
        {"name": "split",
         "transformer": "TrainTestSplitter",
         "params": {"target_column": "purchased"}},
    ],
    "modeling": {"type": "logistic_regression"},
})
pipeline.fit(customers, target_column="purchased")
pipeline.save("customer_model.pkl")
